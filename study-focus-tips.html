How to focus when studying

Nine habits that make focus the default, not a battle.

By the Pomoji team · Updated September 24, 2026

Focusing while studying is less about willpower and more about setup. Most lost study time does not come from one big distraction; it comes from dozens of tiny ones: a notification, a quick check, a tab left open. These nine habits remove the friction so focus becomes the default.

1. Decide what "done" looks like before you start

"Study biology" is a mood. "Summarize chapter 4 into one page of notes" is a task. Write the target down before the timer starts, so you know exactly what you are aiming at.

2. Study in short, timed sessions

Timed sessions, such as 25 minutes of study and a 5-minute break, make it easier to start and easier to keep going. The Pomodoro rhythm also gives you natural checkpoints to notice when your attention drifts.

3. Move your phone out of reach

Research on smartphones and attention suggests that simply having your phone nearby can pull on your focus. Put it in another room or face down across the desk. If you use it as your timer, let the countdown live on the Lock Screen so you never need to unlock it.

4. Use active recall, not rereading

Close the book and try to explain what you just read, or answer practice questions from memory. It feels harder than rereading, and that effort is exactly what makes it stick.

5. Take real breaks

A break that stays on your phone is not a break for your brain. Stand up, stretch, get water or look out of the window. After four sessions, take a longer break of 15 to 30 minutes.

6. Batch the small stuff

Messages, emails and quick questions go on a list and get handled together in a break or at the end. Each one alone seems harmless; together they fracture your attention.

7. Set a daily session goal

Choosing a number, for example four study sessions today, turns a vague plan into something you can track and finish. Finishing it builds momentum for tomorrow.

8. Protect your sleep

Late-night cramming trades tomorrow's focus for tonight's. Sleep is when a lot of learning gets consolidated, so a consistent bedtime is one of the most effective study habits you can build.

9. Review the week, kindly

Once a week, look at when you studied well and when you did not. Patterns, like focusing best in the morning, are more useful than a single bad day.

Frequently asked questions

How long should I study without a break?

Many students do well with 25 to 50 minutes of focus followed by a 5 to 10 minute break. Start with 25 minutes and adjust.

Is the Pomodoro Technique good for studying?

Yes. It makes starting easier, builds in breaks that help you stay fresh, and gives you a simple way to measure study time.
