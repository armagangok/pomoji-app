What to look for in a Pomodoro timer for iPhone

Six features that separate a timer you tolerate from one you keep using.

By the Pomoji team · Updated September 24, 2026

There are hundreds of Pomodoro timers in the App Store, and most of them do the same basic thing: count down from 25 minutes. The differences show up in the small moments: when you lock your phone, when the session ends while you are in another app, or when you want to know whether this week was better than last. Here is what is worth looking for.

1. It keeps counting when you lock the phone

A good timer tracks real time rather than relying on the app staying open. On iPhone, the best experience is a Live Activity: a live countdown on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island, so you can glance at the time without unlocking and without getting pulled into other apps.

2. The end of a session is impossible to miss

Most timers send a normal notification, which is easy to miss when your phone is on silent or in a Focus mode. Since iOS 26, apps can use Apple's AlarmKit to alert like the built-in Clock app: full screen on the Lock Screen and through Silent mode, with the permission you grant. For a Pomodoro timer this is a big deal, because a missed alarm usually means a missed break.

Pomoji rings like an alarm when a session ends, and you can start your break or next session right from the alarm.

3. Breaks are part of the design

The Pomodoro Technique depends on breaks, including the longer break after every four sessions. Look for a timer that tracks the cycle for you and makes the transition from focus to break (and back) a single tap.

4. Honest stats and a daily goal

Stats should help you plan, not guilt you. Useful ones answer simple questions: how many sessions did I finish today, what time of day do I focus best, am I keeping a streak? A daily session goal is a gentle way to turn "work more" into a number you can actually hit.

5. Privacy you do not have to think about

A focus timer does not need your email, your contacts or an advertising ID. Check the App Store privacy label and look for apps that work without an account and keep your history on the device.

6. It makes you want to come back

This one is underrated. The best timer is the one you still open next week. Small touches, a character, a satisfying alarm, a streak worth protecting, make the habit easier to keep.

Frequently asked questions

Does a Pomodoro timer work when the iPhone is locked?

A good one does. Look for apps that track real time and show a Live Activity on the Lock Screen, so the countdown continues even when the app is closed.

Can a Pomodoro app ring when my iPhone is on silent?

On iOS 26 and later, apps that use Apple's AlarmKit can alert like the Clock app, including in Silent mode, once you give them permission. Pomoji supports this.
